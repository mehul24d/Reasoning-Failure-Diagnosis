* **Research question:** RQ4 — what share of first errors is content-attributable, and is
  content attribution predictable from the failure's typology (features of the problem and of
  the trace, without the model's activations)?
* **Labels:** automatic (parser v4).
  - Content-attributable at s0: the distractor is bound at s0, or s0's operation differs from
    gold.
  - **Final E4 results require the S5 agreement record** (`reports/m1/s5_annotation/agreement.json`,
    written by `scripts/annotation_agreement.py`) with kappa ≥ 0.6 (`kappa_min`) for both
    first_wrong_step and error_type. Without it, every output is stamped DEVELOPMENT. If kappa
    falls below 0.6, the output says so, and the result stays descriptive.

## Population

All failures with a first deviating step s0 (parser v4, lenient), both populations (primary
distractor failures and natural failures). Per failure, one row at s0.

## Analyses

1. **Attribution rates (descriptive):** the share of failures that are content-attributable,
   overall, by population and by s0 error type, with template-clustered bootstrap CIs (10,000).
2. **Typology classifier.** It predicts content-attributable from these features, fixed in
   advance **(choice)**:
   - condition (distractor or clean);
   - n steps written;
   - s0 index, and s0 relative to n steps;
   - the question's length in words;
   - the number of numerals in the question;
   - whether s0 is compound.

   s0's error type is **excluded** from the primary classifier because it partly defines the
   label (an operation mismatch is a planning error). A secondary classifier adds it, and is
   flagged as partly definitional.
   - Model: an L2 logistic on standardised features, λ on the E1 grid, chosen by template-grouped
     5-fold CV on train (seed 0). It is fitted on all train failures and scored on the evaluation
     split.
   - **Criterion (for test):** the template-clustered bootstrap CI of the AUROC lies above 0.5.
     Also reported: the shuffled-label null (100 within-template permutations of the train
     labels) and the permutation p = (1 + #≥) / 101.
