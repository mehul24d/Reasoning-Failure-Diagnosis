* **Research question:** RQ3 — at the first deviating step of a failure, is the **gold** value of
  the content variable readable from the model's own residual stream, more often than chance and
  than a prior that ignores the activations?
* **Model:** Gemma-2-2B-IT, float32. Labels: automatic (parser v4); S5 kappa pending.

## Population

- **Primary:** primary (distractor) failures whose first deviating step s0 (parser v4, lenient)
  is **content-attributable**: the distractor quantity is an operand of s0 (variable
  content/binary; gold = "not used", bound = "used"), or s0's operation differs from the aligned
  gold step's operation (content/4-class; gold = gold op, bound = the op written).
- **Secondary:** natural (clean-condition) failures with a content-attributable s0 (operation
  only).
- Read position: s0's first token (`step_first`), the S4 residual cache.
- Split: **dev** (development check, now); **test** (confirmatory, after sign-off and unlock).

## Probes (fitted on the 33 train templates only)

1. **Primary: the E1 probes.** For each content variable, the logistic probe of `prereg/E1.md`:
   E1's train-CV layer and λ (seed 0), E1's equal-size class-stratified train subsample (seed 0).
   These probes are trained on the model's **written** value, so reading the gold value at a
   failure means the activation carries the gold value despite the output.
2. **Secondary: a gold-labelled operation probe** (the October report's amendment: "probes
   trained to separate gold from bound values rather than to predict the output"): the same
   procedure (E1's two-stage CV selection, equal train size, seed 0) on train simple steps
   labelled by `gold_op` instead of `op`. A gold-labelled binary probe is impossible (the gold
   value of "distractor used" is always "not used"), so content/binary has the primary probe
   only.

## Per-failure statistics and baselines

For failure i with gold class g and bound class b, and a probe's class probabilities p:
- `top1_i = [argmax p = g]`
- `gob_i = [p_g > p_b]` (gold over bound; ties count 1/2). For the binary variable, gob = top1.

**INTERPRETATION:**
- **D_V** = mean of top1 over the population (the "top-1 gold" rate that
  `rq3_min_top1_gold` names).
- **G_V** = mean of gob (gold ranked above the value the model wrote).
- **G_shuf** = the same statistic for the 100 shuffled-label probes of E1 (labels permuted within
  template groups, seed 0, same layer and λ); per failure, the mean over the 100.
- **G_prior** = the same statistic for a prior that ignores activations: the train class
  frequencies of the variable at the same step position (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6+). For the binary
  variable this prior always ranks "not used" first, so it is the majority-class baseline that
  the October pilot lacked.

**Criterion (pre-registered for test; applied to dev only as a code check):**
D_V ≥ 0.20 **and** the template-clustered bootstrap CI (10,000 resamples) of **G_V − G_shuf**
and of **G_V − G_prior** both lie above 0. Both differences are paired, per failure. Pooled over
the two variables (primary population); also reported per variable.

**Secondary (descriptive, no gate):**
- Prior-corrected gold over bound: `log p_g − log π_g > log p_b − log π_b`, with π the probe's
  training class frequencies. This removes the reward for the majority class. Reported against
  1/2 and against the shuffled probes with the same correction.
- The bound-value top-1 rate (the probe reads what the model wrote).
- The gold-labelled operation probe's D_V and G_V against the same baselines.
- Stable vs borderline failures. Stable = none of 5 samples at T = 0.7 is correct. The dev labels
  come from the R1 stability samples; the test labels come from S3, when they are unlocked.
- n, templates, ICC, deff and n_eff with every rate (A16).
